About

A screen reader simulator developers can read

screen-reader-mirror turns a page into the flat reading order a screen reader gets, with roles, names and flags, beside the page itself. Open source, MIT, runs in the browser. Who it is for, why it exists, how it works.

Who it is for

Developers and the teams behind them

For the people who ship HTML and never hear it, and the organisations accountable for what they ship.

  • Developers and QASee the reading order while building, before a ticket or an audit finds it.
  • Agencies and outsourced teamsOne pasteable view of what a screen reader gets, so client and vendor look at the same thing.
  • Companies with legal dutiesADA and Section 508 in the US, the European Accessibility Act, WCAG 2.2 AA in procurement. A daily check developers run themselves, not a substitute for an audit.
  • Accessibility specialistsSomething to hand a team that asks "how do we see what you hear", and a place to report where mirror and screen reader disagree.

Why this exists

Speech is hard to picture as HTML

A speech viewer in NVDA or JAWS prints what is spoken. Mapping those words back to the markup takes an expert. Most teams have none, so the check is skipped and linters pass the page anyway.

  • Linters pass itRules see no violation. The order and the words are never checked.
  • Seconds, not daysPaste HTML, choose Run. No install, no account, no learning curve.
  • Nothing leaves your machineWorks on logged in pages behind a VPN. Nothing is sent anywhere.

How the mirror works

Code, page, mirror

Reading order is flat, so the list is flat. Point at the page and the row lights up; focus a row and the element lights up.

  • Speech firstThe Spoken view shows only what a screen reader says. The mirror’s own remarks sit in a dashed note under the row, never in the speech.
  • A flag means worth checkingNot a score, not a verdict. Today: no accessible name, missing alt. More coming.
  • Four viewsSpoken, Preview (the HTML with its CSS removed), Nodes and JSON. How to read them.
  • Then test for realFix the flags, run again, then confirm with a real screen reader and real users.
